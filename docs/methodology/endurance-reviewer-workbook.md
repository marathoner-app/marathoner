## Thank you for considering this review

Marathoner is an early-stage training application intended to help a narrowly
defined group of first-time marathoners follow a clear training plan. Before
the application provides prescriptive plans to beta participants, we want an
independent endurance professional to challenge the proposed methodology.

This is not an endorsement request. Nothing in this workbook is presented as
approved training advice. You may accept a proposal, recommend changes, reject
it, or mark it outside your professional expertise. Marathoner will remain
blocked from using these proposals until the required independent reviews are
complete.

## What we are asking you to review

We are asking for your independent judgment on one proposed completion-focused
plan for adult recreational runners preparing for their first marathon. The
review covers:

- who is sufficiently prepared to enter the plan;
- whether the race is far enough away;
- weekly distance, long-run progression, cutbacks, and tapering;
- the four-run weekly structure and recovery placement;
- deterministic reschedule, hold, repeat, no-change, and refusal decisions
  after completed or missed training;
- the cases the application should decline to support; and
- any missing or ambiguous cases that should block plan generation.

The review does not cover diagnosis, treatment, injury rehabilitation, pain or
unusual-symptom decisions, individualized nutrition, hydration prescriptions,
supplements, pregnancy, youth runners, pace targets, or finish-time promises.
Those subjects are either excluded or require separate qualified review.

## Required adaptation section before delivery

This workbook must not be sent until Marathoner adds the exact missed-workout
and completed-training rules to be reviewed. That section must define:

- the minimum completed-run and perceived-effort inputs;
- the recent-workout pattern required before recommending a change;
- why one difficult workout cannot trigger a material rewrite;
- the precise conditions for reschedule, hold, repeat, no change, or refusal;
- how race feasibility is reassessed after missed training;
- which changes require the runner's explicit approval;
- how a runner can decline without changing the active plan;
- how pain or unusual symptoms are delegated to the separate safety process;
  and
- supported, boundary, ambiguous, and rejected examples for every decision.

Once those rules and examples are authored, this placeholder is replaced by a
reviewable adaptation section and the complete workbook is checked against the
underlying rule definitions before outreach is authorized.

## Intended runner

The current proposal supports only a runner who meets every condition below:

| Proposed condition | Current boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Age and location | Adult, English-speaking, and based in the United States |
| Goal | Complete a first marathon; no pace or finish-time target |
| Recent consistency | At least eight consecutive weeks of consistent running |
| Recent weekly distance | Average 40–45 km (about 25–28 miles) during the previous four weeks |
| Recent frequency | Average four or five running days per week during the previous four weeks |
| Recent long run | 14–20 km (about 9–12.5 miles) during the previous four weeks |
| Availability | The exact four-day pattern required by the proposed plan |
| Race timing | An exact race date 18–22 weeks after the plan begins |
| Safety status | Cleared through a separate, independently reviewed safety process |

If any required condition is missing, conflicting, or outside its approved
range, the application would decline to generate a plan rather than improvise.

## Race timing

The proposal supports an exact race date 18–22 whole weeks after the plan
begins. A race outside that range, an approximate race window, or a missing
date would produce an unsupported result rather than a shortened, extended, or
improvised plan. A race 19–22 weeks away receives one to four repeated
40-kilometer (about 25-mile) lead-in weeks before the 18-week schedule begins.

## Proposed weekly plan

All non-race runs use conversational easy effort. The proposal includes no
intervals, tempo running, hill prescription, heart-rate target, race pace, or
finish-time target. The calendar places four runs around the runner's preferred
long-run day using the same weekly rhythm throughout the plan. For example, if
the long run is Sunday, the other runs fall on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday;
Saturday is the shortest recovery run.

proposed values. Key conversions are shown below for convenient review.

## Progression rules

The plan follows these proposed limits:

- A build week may return to a previously completed high after a cutback.
- A new pre-race weekly-distance high may exceed the previous high by no more
  than 2 km (about 1.25 miles).
- A new pre-race long-run high may exceed the previous high by no more than
  2 km.
- The marathon itself is not treated as a training-load increase that could
  justify later progression.
- The application never adds intensity, a fifth run, or distance beyond the
  reviewed schedule.

### Your assessment

1. Are the post-cutback returns and 2 km new-high limits appropriate?
2. If not, what deterministic replacement would you recommend?
3. What further evidence or testing would you require before use?

## Unsupported and ambiguous cases

The application would decline to generate this plan when the runner is outside
the intended group, has insufficient or conflicting recent training, lacks the
required four-day availability, provides a missing or unsupported race date,
or adds scheduling constraints the fixed plan cannot honor.

Pain, unusual symptoms, rehabilitation, individualized medical questions, and
individualized nutrition questions are not decided through this endurance
review. They remain blocked or referred to the separately qualified review.
