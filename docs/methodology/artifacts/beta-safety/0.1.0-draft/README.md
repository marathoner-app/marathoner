# Founding-beta safety escalation: `0.1.0-draft`

> **Internal draft. Not clinically reviewed. Not medical or training advice. Not
> permitted in participant runtime code.** A qualified reviewer may approve,
> condition, reject, or replace every signal, sentence, and capability block.

## What this draft proves

This packet gives the founders a deterministic internal rehearsal for the
minimum pain and unusual-symptom boundary. It distinguishes a listed emergency
warning sign, another concerning or persistent concern, an unanswered check,
and an explicit no-concern report. It never diagnoses a condition, grades
severity, or says a runner is safe.

Emergency, qualified-help, and incomplete results block new plan generation,
plan adaptation, and training guidance. Read-only training history remains
available. Race timing and a planned progression are deliberately absent from
the evaluator input, so neither can weaken a safety result.

The input contains only enumerated selections. It has no name, account ID,
free-text note, diagnosis, or retention instruction. This rehearsal does not
persist the input or connect it to participant flows.

## Source trace

- The [American Heart Association warning-sign summary](https://www.heart.org/en/about-us/heart-attack-and-stroke-symptoms)
  supports immediate emergency action for chest discomfort, shortness of
  breath, stroke warning signs, loss of responsiveness, and absent normal
  breathing. The exact Marathoner grouping and wording remain product drafts.
- The [CDC heat-illness reference](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html)
  identifies confusion, altered mental status, slurred speech, seizures, and
  loss of consciousness as heat-stroke warning signs requiring emergency care.
- The [CDC athlete heat guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/risk-factors/heat-and-athletes.html)
  tells athletes who feel faint or weak to stop activity and seek care for
  heat-illness symptoms.
- The [NIAMS sports-injury guidance](https://www.niams.nih.gov/health-topics/sports-injuries/diagnosis-treatment-and-steps-to-take)
  says not to work through pain, to stop the provoking activity, and to seek
  care for serious or persistent injury symptoms.

These sources support conservative stop-and-seek-help boundaries. They do not
validate Marathoner's exact categories, copy, data contract, or capability
blocks; those require qualified review before participant use.
